Connect your Tinder in 5 easy steps

Wingman needs the same login credential your browser uses for Tinder. You'll copy it once and paste it into the app. No installs, nothing technical to configure — just follow along.

🧠 What is this token, really?

When you log into Tinder on the web, Tinder gives your browser a 36-character login code so you stay signed in. It's the key to your account — so Wingman treats it like a password: you paste it into the app yourself, it stays in memory only, and you should never share it with anyone.

1

Open Tinder in your browser and log in

Go to tinder.com/app and sign in the way you normally do (Google, phone number, or email). You must be fully logged in — you should see your swipe deck.

Open the browser's inspection tools

Right-click anywhere on the Tinder page and choose Inspect. A panel opens — that's your browser's toolbox for developers. It works in Chrome, Edge, and Firefox.

3

Find “Application” → “Local Storage”

At the top of the inspector there's a row of tabs. Click Application (in Firefox: Storage). If you don't see it, click the ≫ button — hidden tabs live there.

Under Local Storage → https://tinder.com you'll see a small table of rows. You're looking for the row named exactly TinderWeb/APIToken.

4

Copy the token value

Click the value on the TinderWeb/APIToken row (the long code on the right, not the name). It's 36 characters with dashes, like:

bff5d171-23a4-4c8e-9f2a-6d1b0c7e4a58

Double-click the value to select all of it, then Ctrl+C (or ⌘+C). Copy the whole thing — all 36 characters.

5

Paste it into Wingman

Open the Wingman app, paste the code into the Connect box, and press Connect. You should see a green “Connected as <your name>” — that's it, you're in. 🎉

Good to know

How long does the token stay valid?

From our live testing: the token stayed valid across hours of use and multiple page refreshes. It's invalidated by logging out of Tinder on the web, and Tinder may rotate it eventually. Wingman tells you clearly when it stops working — just come back and copy a fresh one (same 5 steps).

Is this safe? What does Wingman do with it?

The token goes from your paste to the app's memory — it is never saved to a file or database, never logged, never shown in full again (only a masked fingerprint like bff5…9f31), and never sent anywhere except Tinder's own API. Press Disconnect and it's gone instantly.

I can't find the TinderWeb/APIToken row

Make sure you're on tinder.com/app and actually logged in, then refresh the page and re-open Local Storage. In Firefox the path is Storage → Local Storage. If you see other TinderWeb/… rows but not APIToken, you're not logged in — log in first.

Wingman says the token is invalid or expired

Most likely Tinder ended the session (this happens after logging out or long gaps). Log into tinder.com again and copy a fresh token — steps 1–5, two minutes.

Can someone else use my token?

Yes — that's why it must be treated like a password. Anyone holding it can use your account, so never paste it into sites you don't trust or send it to anyone. Wingman runs locally on your machine, so the token stays with you.
