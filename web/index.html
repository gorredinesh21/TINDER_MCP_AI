In memory only. Your token lives in the app's session memory — never in a file, database, log, or analytics.

Questions, answered

What is the token, and why does Wingman need it?

When you log into Tinder on the web, your browser keeps a login credential (a 36-character code). Wingman uses that same credential to read your account — exactly like your browser does. Without it, Wingman can't see your profile.

Where do I get the token?

Open tinder.com logged in, open the browser's inspection tools, and copy one value from storage. It takes about 2 minutes — the step-by-step visual guide shows exactly where.

Is my token stored anywhere?

No. It lives in the app's memory for your session and is dropped when you disconnect, log out, or after 2 idle hours. It is never written to disk, never logged, and never sent to anything except Tinder's own API.

How does the AI work?

Wingman runs on Google Cloud's Gemini models via Vertex AI. Your profile and conversations are sent to Gemini to produce analyses and drafts — nothing is retained by the model.

Can the AI send messages for me?

It can draft them — but sending is always your call. Every message goes: AI drafts → you edit if you like → you press Send. There is no auto-send anywhere in the product, by design.

Can it swipe for me?

Not today. Swiping is technically researched but deliberately not enabled until it's verified safe and reliable — see the honest status in Docs → Technical.

What happens when the token expires?

Tinder tokens eventually stop working (for example when you log out). Wingman detects it, tells you in plain words, and asks you to paste a fresh one. Nothing breaks silently.

Can I disconnect my account?

Any time — one click drops the token from memory immediately. You can also log out of Tinder on the web to invalidate the token everywhere.
