# Dating-Profile Knowledge Pack — India (2025–2026)

> This file is the "taught knowledge" the AI uses to (a) score and standardize a Tinder
> profile and (b) draft messages. It is loaded into the model as reference context.
> Every rule below is phrased so it can be checked or applied programmatically.
> Sources are listed at the bottom; figures are directional (from public studies/surveys),
> not guarantees.

---

## 0. Operating principles (read first)

1. **Authenticity over optimization.** Never invent facts about the user (job, height,
   hobbies, travel). Rewrite *how* things are said, never fabricate *what* is true.
2. **The user is the only sender.** For messages, the AI drafts; the human reviews and
   sends. Never auto-send to a match. (Design constraint, not a style tip.)
3. **Respect the other person.** No manipulation, negging, pressure, or sexual content in
   openers. In the Indian market this is not just ethics — it's effective (see §3).
4. **Specific beats clever.** A concrete, true detail outperforms a generic witty line.

## 1. PHOTO RUBRIC

Photos drive ~70% of the swipe decision and matter more than the bio. Order and variety
matter more than raw attractiveness of any single shot.

### Hard rules (score-killers if violated)
- **P1 — First photo = solo, clear face, well-lit, no sunglasses/hat covering face.**
  The primary photo controls the majority of swipes. Never lead with a group shot.
- **P2 — No group photo in the first slot**, and ideally not in slot 2 either. If a group
  photo is used, it must be obvious which person is the user, and only after 3+ strong solos.
- **P3 — Max one selfie.** Mirror/selfie shots score materially lower; prefer photos taken
  by someone else.
- **P4 — 4–6 photos total.** Profiles with 4–6 photos beat 1–2-photo profiles significantly.
  Fewer than 3 is a major gap; more than 6 adds little.

### Variety target (the "photo set")
Aim for this spread across the 4–6 slots:
1. Clear solo headshot/portrait (slot 1)
2. Full-body shot (shows build/style honestly)
3. Activity / hobby shot (sport, music, hiking, cooking — shows lifestyle)
4. Social proof shot (with friends — clearly identifiable) OR travel shot
5. Optional: pet / candid laughing / something that invites a question

### Soft signals (improve score)
- Genuine smile / eye contact in at least one photo.
- Good natural lighting; avoid heavy filters.
- One photo that gives an obvious conversation hook (an instrument, a dog, a mountain).
- India context: a clean, "presentable but not try-hard" first photo reads well; overly
  flashy/luxury-flex shots can read as inauthentic to the serious-intent segment.

### Photo scoring (0–100)
- Slot-1 is solo + clear face + good light: 30 pts (all-or-nothing on the "solo+clear" part)
- Has full-body shot: 15
- Has activity/lifestyle shot: 15
- Photo count in 4–6 range: 15
- ≤1 selfie: 10
- Variety (≥3 distinct *types* of shot): 15
- Penalties: group photo in slot 1 (−40), >1 selfie (−10 each), all photos same setting (−15)

## 2. BIO RUBRIC

A profile with a bio gets multiples more matches than an empty one — but a *bad* bio can
hurt. Target the sweet spot.

### Hard rules
- **B1 — Length 100–300 characters.** Shorter generally beats longer; never a wall of text.
- **B2 — No clichés that describe anyone** ("love to travel, foodie, fluent in sarcasm,
  partner in crime, here for a good time"). Flag and replace.
- **B3 — At least one *specific, true* detail** (a real hobby, a niche interest, a concrete
  preference). Generic = weak.
- **B4 — End with a hook / conversation prompt** (a question, an opinion to react to, a
  this-or-that). Gives matches something to reply to.
- **B5 — No negativity / lists of demands** ("no drama", "swipe left if…", "don't waste my
  time"). Reads as bitter; especially penalized by women in the India safety-first segment.

### Structure that works (template the AI can fill from true facts)
```
[1 line: a specific, slightly playful identity statement — what you actually do / are into]
[1 line: 1–2 concrete interests or a light, true quirk]
[1 line: a hook — a question, a debate-starter, or what you're looking for]
```
Example shape (fill with the user's real facts; do not copy verbatim):
> Data engineer who treats weekend treks like prod incidents — over-prepared and oddly happy.
> Will judge you (lovingly) on your chai-to-coffee ratio.
> Best unpopular food opinion wins a first date. Go.

### Tone calibration for India
- Confident + warm + lightly humorous tends to win. Humor signals intelligence and
  low-stakes confidence.
- Avoid over-sharing or intense romantic declarations up front.
- Signaling *intent* honestly helps in 2025+ (many users are tired of ambiguity / "swipe
  fatigue"). If the user wants something serious, a light honest signal beats hiding it.
- Respectful framing > edgy framing. The market rewards "respect & safety."

### Bio scoring (0–100)
- Length in 100–300 chars: 20
- Has ≥1 specific true detail: 25
- Has a conversation hook/prompt: 20
- Free of clichés: 15
- Positive/respectful tone (no demands/negativity): 20
- Penalties: cliché phrases (−5 each), negativity/demands (−20), empty bio (score = 0)

## 3. INDIA MARKET CONTEXT (shapes tone + strategy)

- **Scale & cities:** ~42% of urban Indian singles have used a dating app; Tinder is the
  most-downloaded, strongest in Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune. Bumble strong
  among urban professional women 22–35 (women message first).
- **Safety is the #1 filter for women.** ~53% of young women immediately unmatch on an
  inappropriate message. ~44% prioritize *verified* profiles. → Tell the user to verify
  their profile; keep openers clean and respectful.
- **What women say they value:** keeping promises (51%), undivided attention/real
  conversation (36%), respectful language (31%). → Lead with genuine interest, not flattery.
- **Hybrid culture:** users blend modern dating with traditional expectations; many keep
  app use private. Don't assume; don't push. Match the other person's pace and openness.
- **Shift to intentional dating:** 75%+ Gen Z report "swiping fatigue"; "serious" positioning
  is growing. Authenticity and clear (gentle) intent outperform pure banter.
- **Language:** English works in metros; a light sprinkle of Hindi/regional phrasing can
  build rapport *if* it matches how the user actually talks — never forced.

## 4. MESSAGE / OPENER RUBRIC (drafts only — human sends)

### Hard rules
- **M1 — Personalize to THIS match.** Reference a specific detail from her bio/photos
  (a trip, a pet, an instrument, a job, a verification badge, an anthem). Generic "hey/hi"
  is banned — it's the worst-performing opener.
- **M2 — Length 5–15 words** for an opener. Short and easy to answer.
- **M3 — Give them something to reply to** — a light question or a playful observation, not
  a statement that dead-ends.
- **M4 — Use her name** when known (raises reply rate).
- **M5 — Never:** sexual content, generic flattery ("you're so beautiful"), pickup-line
  clichés, copied meme lines, or anything pressuring. These trigger unmatch/report in the
  India safety-first segment.
- **M6 — Match register & pace.** Mirror her message length and energy as the chat
  develops; don't escalate intimacy faster than she does.

### Opener pattern
`[reference a specific detail] + [light question that invites a story]`
> "Okay the Hampi photo wins your profile — was that sunrise worth the 4am alarm?"
> "Fellow filter-coffee loyalist? Or are you secretly a Starbucks person I have to forgive?"

### Reply drafting (mid-conversation)
- Read the last few turns; keep continuity; ask follow-ups about things she raised.
- Move toward a low-pressure plan (a specific, easy date idea) only once there's clear
  mutual engagement — never on message 1–2.
- Always produce 2–3 *options* of different tones (playful / sincere / curious) for the
  human to choose from, plus a one-line "why this works." The human decides and sends.

### Message scoring (0–100) — for evaluating a draft before showing it
- References a specific detail of the match: 35
- Length 5–15 words (opener): 15
- Contains an answerable question/hook: 20
- Respectful, non-sexual, non-generic: 20
- Uses name when available: 10
- Hard-fail (score 0): sexual, pressuring, or "hey/hi"-only

## 5. SAFETY, ETHICS & PLATFORM GUARDRAILS (always on)

- **Disclosure to matches is the human's call, but the human is always the sender.** The AI
  must never be configured to silently auto-send messages to real people.
- **No deception about identity or facts.** Don't fabricate the user's life.
- **Respect unmatch / disinterest signals.** If a match goes quiet or sets a boundary,
  the AI must not draft persistent or pressuring follow-ups.
- **Platform ToS reality:** automated *actions* against Tinder (bulk auto-swipe, auto-send)
  risk account bans. Keep automation to read + draft; keep a human in the loop for sends.
- **Minors / safety:** never assist with anyone who may be underage; flag and stop.
